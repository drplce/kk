The Studio

Three small games built from what the essay says about Pigman Fat’s quiz shows: a score to reach, a job to find and a venture to keep afloat.

Read this first

These are toys, not reconstructions. The essay describes the shows in a few lines each, and no rules, questions or recordings survive on this site. Every question, job, number and rule below is this site’s own, written to fit the essay’s description. The essay is by DTW and is reported in chapter XVIII.

Game two

Find the Job

Twenty questions to name an occupation. Asking and guessing both cost a question. In Fat’s rules the essay says jobs were “changed at will”, so try both ways of playing.

Questions left 20

Ask

Guess the job

    Choose your rules, then ask.

    Fat’s rules here work like this: the quizmaster has not picked a job, and answers each question so that as many jobs as possible stay possible. A guess is wrong until only one job is left. That is this site’s reading of “flexible rules”, not a record of how the real show ran. The jobs and the answers about them are the site’s own.

    Game three

    Venture

    You run a cash-strapped quiz, as the essay says Mik Boaty did. Eight rounds. Fair questions cost prize money. The Unlucky Dip pays you the amount a contestant nominates, at once, and listeners notice. Pass the hat at half time.

      All numbers are the site’s own. The essay says only that the programme was cash-strapped, that contestants who ended with negative scores paid the debt on leaving, that the Unlucky Dip lost a contestant the amount nominated, and that a scorer took a hat round the audience at half time. How these fit together here is invented.
