Vol. III · The Presenter

X Presenter

Rafe Lavine

The man reading the names. Three decades of South African radio across five stations, a rock specialist who also ran the country's best-loved jazz hour, and the voice on this tape.

Trade
Radio presenter, 1983 to 2014, then voice artist
Stations
Radio 702 · Radio 5 · SAfm · Radio Today · Talk Radio 702
Specialism
Classic and progressive rock, with a sideline in jazz and soul
The voice
South African, warm, conversational, built for documentary
Company
Leon Economides, Chris Prior, David Blood, Phil Wright
Now
Dawlish, on the south Devon coast, retired

Career, as far as it could be traced

  1. 1983Radio 702. Cuts his teeth on the independent station, three years old at the time, presenting a show called Rock ’n Horror.
  2. 1984Radio 5, SABC. Moves to the national pop station and presents Hot Dog for six years, alongside a roster that included Alex Jay, Kevin Savage and Chris Prior.
  3. 1987SABC TV1. Appears on Pop Shop, the television pop programme, as one of the Radio 5 DJs. The clip survives on YouTube.
  4. 1990Off air. His Radio 5 contract ends. He manages music stores, then a music distribution company, and by his own account even tries his hand at selling assurance.
  5. 1990sSAfm. Back at the microphone on the SABC's English talk and music station with Rock Classics and the Great Unknowns, where he first shares a studio with Leon Economides, and The Quiet Storm, jazz and soul on Sunday nights from nine until midnight. The mystery-song feature on this tape, and Kobus Kennedy's call, belong to his SAfm years.
  6. 2003The Rock IT Scientists. Moved to SAfm's late slot, ten at night until one, he rings Economides and proposes a Friday co-presented show on the Rock Classics model: progressive, hard and classic rock, blues, brass rock, jazz and metal. “Rafe is the ultimate professional and a pleasure to work with,” Economides says a year in. “I really consider myself lucky.”
  7. 2004Axed. In the last week of September the SABC cuts almost all of SAfm's weekend programmes. The Quiet Storm is heard for the last time.
  8. 2006The RockFest. With Prior, Blood, Wright and Economides he founds a block of rock programming on the community station Radio Today, 1485 AM. It lasts about three and a half years, until the station stops. When RockFest resurfaces on Mix 93.8 FM in November 2009, he is not among the two who carry on.
  9. 2010sTalk Radio 702, again. Back where he began, presenting the weekend Solid Gold slot from four to seven in the evening.
  10. 2014Leaves South Africa. In December he signs off from Solid Gold to join his family in the United Kingdom.
  11. NowDawlish, Devon. Retired from radio after almost thirty years. Listed as a voice artist for commercial and documentary work, with the accent he took with him.
Rock music around the world, but particularly in this country over the last couple of years, has been neglected. There is a huge market out there, a more mature and affluent market that has not been catered for. Rafe Lavine, March 2004
The umbrella under which I do my show is entertainment. Many of the younger people out there are getting tired of listening to artists that are just giving them re-worked versions of classic songs, and they are interested in where the original material comes from. Rafe Lavine, March 2004
Leon is a certified lunatic. The amount of money he has spent on music over the years is incredible, but he has always had a passion for music and it's always been quality. Rafe Lavine on Leon Economides, his co-presenter
His shows were like an institution. Very informative, and you could tell he did his research and knew much more about the music. Concord Nkabinde, bassist, on The Quiet Storm

Ten records, in his own order

Asked in 2004 for the albums that made him, Lavine gave this list, with the remarks that follow each one. Seven of the ten are from the same decade as the mystery song, and one of them is the record that got him into jazz.

  1. 1Led Zeppelin IV 1971“A life-changing experience. When Black Dog leapt out of the speakers, I couldn't believe what I was hearing. A benchmark for hard rock.”
  2. 2Quincy Jones Smackwater Jack 1971“A ground-breaking album. Funk, jazz, rock and Q's legendary arranging skills fuse into a must-have album. The extended version of Marvin Gaye's What's Going On is a landmark.”
  3. 3AC/DC Highway to Hell 1979“No-nonsense, down-to-earth hard, grinding, tough boogie. And Bon Scott's vocals cut through armourplate!”
  4. 4The Peddlers Three in a Cell 1968“Unsung heroes, this trio of Brit musicians. Have something of a cult following here in SA. This was the album that got me into jazz in a big way.”
  5. 5Donald Fagen The Nightfly 1982“The Dan's wry irony transferred seamlessly into this slick, sassy, rootsy milestone.”
  6. 6Pink Floyd The Dark Side of the Moon 1973“Words are superfluous!”
  7. 7Greatest Show on Earth The Going's Easy 1970“A classic example of why Brit prog rock is so highly regarded, with a razor sharp horn section. An album one never tires of listening to.”
  8. 8Livin' Blues Blue Breeze 1975“From Holland. THE blues-rock album of the 20th century. Passion, fire, evocative songs. Pure Nirvana!”
  9. 9Maynard Ferguson MF Horn 2 1972“A horn section that will simply blow your head off, if you can find it. Not your head, the album!”
  10. 10Dream Theater Metropolis Pt. 2: Scenes from a Memory 1999“The ultimate concept album. Mind-boggling arrangements, stupefying musicianship and a chilling tale, with a South African connection: Kevin ‘Caveman’ Shirley.”

Not one of the ten is Pick Up the Pieces. But a man whose second-favourite record is Quincy Jones fusing funk, jazz and rock, and whose ninth is a big band with a horn section that blows your head off, is exactly the man to hand a nine-second Average White Band riff to a country and dare it to name the tune.

His SAfm programmes

Four can be documented. The tape's “this afternoon” fits none of them, so the mystery-song feature most likely ran on an earlier daytime programme from his first SAfm years, before the late slot.

  1. Rock Classics and the Great Unknowns1990sThe show on which he first worked with Leon Economides. Classic rock alongside records nobody else would play. Its format was revived in 2003 as the Rock IT Scientists.
  2. The Quiet StormSundays, 21:00 to midnightJazz, soul and smooth late-night music, the programme listeners remember him for most. Axed in the SABC's weekend cull, last heard in September 2004. A smooth-jazz compilation CD bearing the programme's name and his was later circulated, though no catalogue entry for it could be found.
  3. The late slotWeeknights, 22:00 to 01:00The 2003 radio guide lists it simply as “Rafe Lavine”, with no programme title. Three hours of music five nights a week, which is where the Friday rock show lived.
  4. The Rock IT ScientistsFridays, from 2003Co-presented with Economides: progressive, hard and classic rock, blues, brass rock, Latin and African rock, jazz and metal. The show Lavine described as coming under “the umbrella of entertainment”.

What was checked

  • ✓NameRafe Lavine, with an a. Remembered as Levine, and the confusion is old: a 2003 newspaper radio guide printed “The Quiet Storm with Rafe Levine” in the same edition that spelt him Lavine in the evening slot. Every professional listing uses Lavine.
  • ✓StationSAfm, the SABC's national English station. Formerly Radio South Africa, renamed SAfm in 1995. The family's recollection, and consistent with Lavine's documented SAfm years. An earlier draft of this page guessed Radio 5 and was wrong.
  • ✓Years1990s to September 2004. The window in which Lavine presented on SAfm, which brackets the call. The Quiet Storm was last heard in the weekend cull of 2004.
  • ?ShowBeat the Beat is how the family remembers the feature, and the clue's “if you got beat yesterday” fits a daily game by that name. No printed source names it, and no source records which SAfm programme carried it.
  • ?DateThe tape carries no date. 1974 is the year of the record, not of the broadcast.

Not found, and not invented

  • –BirthNo date or place of birth appears in any source. He was on air by 1983, so he was born no later than the early 1960s.
  • –SlotThe tape says “this afternoon”. His documented SAfm slots in 2003 were weeknights at ten and Sunday at nine, so this is either an earlier daytime programme or a weekend one that the schedules found do not show.
  • –FamilyThe 2014 report says only that he left to join his family in the UK. Nothing more is published, and nothing more is asked.
  • –This callWhether he remembers a first-time caller from Cape Town naming an Average White Band record is unknown. He is, as far as the record shows, reachable in Devon.
