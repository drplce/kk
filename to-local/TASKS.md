# Tasks for the local session

Context. The site (https://drplce.github.io/kk/) has an interactive radio dial: pick a year, a city,
AM or FM, and see which South African station sat on which frequency, with marks on years where
something changed. It is gaining time of day and day of week, so a visitor can see which show each
station ran at a given hour. The cloud session reached the web only through search-result summaries,
and it ran out of its search allowance: every direct page fetch failed (en.wikipedia.org, oldradio.com,
voices.com, iol.co.za, discogs.com, esat.sun.ac.za, mg.co.za and others were blocked or did not resolve).
You can open pages. Please do.

Work in this order. For each task, write one file under `from-local/` as described in `FORMAT.md`.

## P1. Frequencies by year (the biggest hole)

1. World Radio TV Handbook and its predecessors on worldradiohistory.com (the "Archive-WRTH" and
   "Archive-Guide-to-Broadcasting" folders; also the "International World Radio Station List" of 1951).
   For South Africa, for 1951, 1961, 1965, 1970, 1975, 1980, 1985, 1990, 1995, 2000, 2005, 2010 (the
   nearest edition available), extract every medium-wave (kHz) and FM (MHz) entry: station or service
   name, city or transmitter site, frequency, power, and the page. Priority stations: Springbok Radio,
   SABC English Service, Radio South Africa, SAfm, Radio 5 / 5FM, Radio 2000, Radio Highveld, Radio Good
   Hope, Radio Port Natal, Radio Orion, Radio Lotus, Metro FM, Radio 702, Capital Radio 604, Radio Today
   1485, CapeTalk 567, Radio Pulpit 657, Jacaranda, Kfm, East Coast Radio, Radio Algoa, Radio Oranje,
   Kaya FM, YFM, Classic FM, Radio Bop. Write the frequencies exactly as printed.
2. The Wikipedia pages for: Springbok Radio, SAfm, 5FM, Radio 702, Capital Radio 604, 947, Metro FM,
   Radio 2000, East Coast Radio, Good Hope FM, Kfm 94.5, Radio Orion, Lotus FM, Jacaranda FM, Algoa FM,
   OFM, YFM, Kaya FM, Hot 1027, Radio Pulpit, Brixton Tower, Media in Pretoria, Radio in South Africa,
   History of broadcasting in South Africa. Record each page's infobox frequencies, launch and rename
   dates, and the footnoted source behind each date.
3. The ICASA and IBA documents: AnnexA-K_BroadcastingFrequencies-Q12013.pdf on icasa.org.za, the IBA
   medium-wave plan on polity.org.za (1999), and any ICASA frequency or licence list that gives a
   station's frequency by city. Record frequency, city, licence dates.
4. oldradio.com/archives/international/safrica.html, radioheritage.com story387 and "100 years of radio in
   South Africa", the SABC Media Libraries blog (sabcmedialib.blogspot.com: the Springbok Radio series and
   "Between stations"), and ESAT pages (Springbok Radio, SAfm, Radio South Africa, Radio 2000, Radio Today,
   Heart FM). Record every frequency and date, with the page and URL.

## P2. Programme schedules (time of day and day of week)

5. Springbok Radio: weekly or daily programme schedules for any year (SABC Media Libraries posts, the
   Springbok Radio Preservation Society, Wikipedia's programme lists, old-time-radio sites). Record
   programme, day, start and end time, years on air, host. Pin down the weekday block pattern
   (breakfast 05:00 to 08:00 or 08:30, women's, Afrikaans serials, children's, news 19:00, family shows)
   to the decade it describes. Check whether the quiz titles "21", "Venture", "Just the Job",
   "Find the Job", "Skip show 22" can be corroborated, and the names O'Bryne, Shippey, Mik Boaty,
   Le'vil Causson, and a producer surnamed Fat nicknamed "Pigman". Also Squad Cars, Taxi!, Pick a Box,
   Challenge of Space, The Avengers: day, time, years.
6. Web Archive snapshots (web.archive.org) of safm.co.za schedule pages, 5fm.co.za, 702.co.za, 947.co.za,
   metrofm.co.za, goodhopefm.co.za, kfm.co.za, ecr.co.za and radiotoday.co.za (any years from 1998 to
   2015). Record weekday, Saturday and Sunday line-ups with start and end times.
7. Newspaper radio guides if you can reach them: IOL "DJ's patrol hits Durban" (November 2003, the SAfm
   schedule), IOL "Gotta keep on rocking" (March 2004), Mail & Guardian radio coverage (SAfm PM Live:
   1998 5 to 7pm? 2000 weekdays 4 to 6pm?), Bizcommunity line-up changes at Talk Radio 702 (2012 to 2014),
   and Radio Orion's schedule (1983 to 1993).
8. Rafe Lavine's shows: Hot Dog (Radio 5), Rock Classics and the Great Unknowns, The Quiet Storm (Sundays
   21:00 to midnight until September 2004), the 2003 weeknight 22:00 to 01:00 slot, The Rock IT Scientists
   (Fridays, SAfm), The RockFest (Radio Today 1485 AM, then 93.8 FM), Solid Gold (Talk Radio 702).
   Find "PM Jive" and "Beat the Beat" on SAfm: weekdays 14:00 to 16:00, Beat the Beat at 14:20 and answers
   at 14:45, years unknown (reported by a former caller; no print source found). Pages: rock.co.za/rockit,
   sarockmusic.com, sugarmusicnews.wordpress.com (The History of RockFest Radio),
   rockfestonmix938fm.wordpress.com.

## P5. Pigman Fat (added 24 October 2026)

The owner recalls two things about the quiz producer in the essay "The Battle of the airwaves" (surname
Fat, said "Fett", nicknamed "Pigman"): an Afrikaans accent, not strong but distinctive, and an unverified
rumour of a brother called "Pieman" (possibly just a nickname). Please look, and report only what a
source says:

16. Any South African radio or quiz figure nicknamed Pieman, Pigman, Pig Man, or similar, with years and
    station. Any quiz producer or presenter surnamed Fat or Vet, or described as "the Pigman". Try radio
    memory sites, the Springbok Radio Preservation Society, SABC Media Libraries, forum threads (for
    example the 4x4community "Springbokradio" thread), old newspapers, and Facebook or blog nostalgia
    groups you can reach. Record the exact wording and URL.
17. Any account of how he spoke (an accent, an Afrikaans-English mix), and any account of a brother.
    If a source describes him, record it as written, with the date and the person who wrote it.
    Do not infer a name, a relationship or a trade the source does not state.
18. The origin of the surname Fat, and whether it is a respelling of Afrikaans "vet" (pronounced "fett").
    Genealogy and surname dictionaries only if they actually say so.

Use the same format as the schedule files, with the programme column left blank, or a plain notes file
`from-local/T16-pigman.md` if the findings are not tabular. Say "not found" when it is not found.
