    Notes on the dial

    What the dial knows, and what it does not

    Every frequency, year and programme on the tuner carries a source number and a confidence mark. Most of it is thin. This is how it was found and where it stops.

    Two rounds of research ran for this instrument: five agents on frequencies and stations, then seven on programme schedules, split by day-part and by day. About two hundred web searches were made in all, and every attempt to open a page failed, because the sites were blocked or did not resolve. So everything here comes from search-result summaries, most of them of Wikipedia pages or their mirrors, and quotations are the search tool’s rendering, not the pages’ own words. Two searches that agree may therefore be one source twice, and the confidence marks say so. The schedule round ran out of its shared search allowance after a few searches each, so schedules are the thinnest layer: for most stations and years the dial says “no programme found” and means exactly that.

    Where only a current frequency could be found, it is shown for 2026 alone. Where a station is known but its frequency is not, it appears in a separate row without a dial position, so nothing is placed on the dial that was not found. Frequencies on Pigman’s dial are decoration.

    Not found, and not invented

    • –SpringbokNo medium-wave frequency for Springbok Radio in any city or year. Its pre-1976 and 1976 to 1985 dial is blank.
    • –SABC AMNo medium-wave frequency for the English Service, Radio South Africa, Radio 5 or Good Hope, and no SAfm frequency before the current listing. The only SABC medium-wave switch-off found is Radio Metro 576 in June 2011.
    • –Regional FMHighveld before 1988, Good Hope, Port Natal and East Coast, Jacaranda and Radio Orion: no frequency by year, only current ranges for some.
    • –PM JiveReported by Trevor Worley as weekdays 2 to 4 on SAfm, with Beat the Beat at 2.20 and its answers at 2.45. Searches found no listing under either name. It is shown as years-unknown, never placed on a year.
    • –The essayOf the essay’s shows, only the titles 21 and Venture were found, as Springbok Radio quiz titles. No hosts, times or years for any of them, and nothing for Skip show 22, Find the Job, Mik Boaty, O’Bryne and Shippey, or Pigman Fat.
    • –CitiesOnly Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban are offered, and the Cape Town and Durban data is the sparsest.
    • –NextThe sources that would fill most of this are blocked from where this was built: the World Radio TV Handbook and station-list archives, ICASA frequency annexes, the Web Archive’s old schedule pages, and radio guides. A second pass from an open connection is planned.

    Corrections to earlier pages

    • ✓RockFestThe presenter chapter said The RockFest on Radio Today lasted “until the station stops”. It was ended by the station’s management in February 2009, per its host’s history, and Radio Today did not stop. It also said the move to 93.8 FM was in November 2009; the history says early December. Both are now corrected there.

    The true origin, in brief

    Who was Pigman Fat?

    From “The Battle of the airwaves”, an essay by DTW

    A quiz producer called Fat, nicknamed Pigman. Fat was his surname, said Fett, and the essay thinks Pigman was earned by his appearance, manners and customs. He copied the programmes that worked, and changed them: Skip show 22 from 21, Find the Job from Just the Job.

    Then he took over a rival’s Venture with soft drink money, outlasted a competitor who sent a scorer round the audience with a hat, and kept the radio quizzes going while television soaked up the sponsors. Listeners wrote to the papers. Few of them were kind.

    The essay ends on his name. Pigman Fat (or PMF as he is sometimes known), has become famous, some would say notorious. His moniker is also a by-word for piggishness.
