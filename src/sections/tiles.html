Who set the values

Every search extract agrees on the lineage. The game began as Lexiko, was renamed Criss-Crosswords, and in 1948 James Brunot of Newtown, Connecticut, bought the manufacturing rights and renamed it Scrabble. Its inventor, Alfred Mosher Butts, drew the tile frequencies from letter counts on the front page of the New York Times, and each letter carried a number, with rarer letters worth more. One extract from the Strong National Museum of Play says the original values have stayed put while the dictionary changed. Brunot’s rename is dated 1948 in two extracts. Butts’s year of invention is not agreed (see the open rows below).

What no extract supplied is the reason a K is worth five, a Y four, a B three and a D two. The general rule, rarer means dearer, is all the sources give. So the K at the head of each half of the name is five points because it is rare in English, and this chapter will not pretend to a better reason than that.

One K in the box

A search extract for the standard English distribution gives one K tile, two B, four D and two Y, in a set of 100 tiles, 98 letters and two blanks. The name holds two K’s. A single set cannot spell it. The second K must be a blank, which scores nothing, and the name falls from 26 to 21. The instrument below does this arithmetic for you: it marks any surplus letter as a blank. The tile counts for the other letters in the name (E, N, O, U, S) were not read from a page in this run, so the instrument uses the standard set from memory and the page flags that as unchecked.

Chapter V lists the 26 in a table of what the name “adds up to.” That figure treats every tile as free and every letter as available. It is the number a person would get with a pencil, not a number a Scrabble board would ever pay.

The board

The standard board is fifteen squares each way, 225 in all, with 61 premium squares: 24 double-letter, 12 triple-letter, 17 double-word (one of them the centre star) and 8 triple-word. Two extracts, from Wikipedia and from the Mattel instruction sheet, give those counts and agree. The first word must cross the centre star, which counts as a double word. A premium counts only when a tile is first laid on it, a blank scores nothing even on a premium, two word premiums multiply, and seven tiles played in one turn earn a bonus of 50 points. The layout used by the instrument below is the standard symmetric one, written out by hand and checked only against those counts (24, 12, 17, 8), not against a published coordinate table, because none could be reached.

KENNEDY is the interesting row. It is seven letters, exactly one rack, so it would earn the 50-point bonus, and across the middle row from D8 it covers a double letter under the K and the star. That is 90 for an opening, and it is not a word. For scale, an extract from Wikipedia gives the highest opening on one word list as BEZIQUE for 124, and Mental Floss gives MUZJIKS at 126 or 128, so those two sources disagree and the page leaves it open. The best opening for KOBUS is 32, and the name’s own K, at D8 on the double-letter square, supplies ten of those points.

Which words are words

Proper nouns are not playable, and one rules summary says so in those words, with the caveat that a word that is also a dictionary word can be valid. Kobus and Kennedy are names, so neither is a play. Their parts are another matter. A Merriam-Webster Scrabble finder lists KOB, the reddish-brown antelope of the genus that chapter XIV set apart from the given name, and its plural KOBS, as playable on a North American word list. The same finder is reported to list KENNED. KOBO was found only inside another word’s anagram list. Nothing at all was found for KEN, KENS, KENNEL, KOBUS or KOBUSES, which is not proof that they are invalid. No Collins Scrabble Words check of any of them could be made.

The same name on other tiles

Values follow how common a letter is, so they change with the language. Extracts of the Wikipedia article on letter distributions give Dutch tiles as K3, B3, U4, O1 and S2, and German K3, B3 and U1. On those Dutch values Kobus would come to 13 against the English 11, but the same extracts disagree about Y in Dutch, and no full table for any language other than English could be read. Those figures are extracts, not checked values. One extract says the first Afrikaans edition was named Krabbel, made by Leon Toys of Johannesburg, with 100 tiles. Its K value and tile count were not found, so the Afrikaans score of the first name cannot be given.

Scrabble at home

The extracts about South African Scrabble are thin and mostly undated. They say Anita Kassel started the Thornton Scrabble Club in Cape Town in 1981 and founded the Western Province affiliate in June 1987, and that Gwen Heiman led the Johannesburg club and the formation of what became Scrabble SA. One extract dates the first Johannesburg club to the 1960s, another puts the national body in the late 1980s. A 2020 column in the SAJR is reported to say South African words such as INDABA, UBUNTU and NKOSI are allowable. Whether BRAAI, VELD or BOEREWORS are is not known. Llewellin Jegels was crowned national champion in 2013, per a Rhodes University extract.
