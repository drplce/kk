Editions

How this site grows

Every edition keeps the bones and changes the body. Once a week, on its own, the site chooses one thing already on it that deserves a deeper look, sends out research, builds what comes back into a new or expanded section, changes its visual treatment, and adds one interactive element. The log below is the record.

  5. V

    Criteria

    14 September 2026

    Pick Up the Pieces followed to the room it was cut in: a dropped MCA contract, a party in Laurel Canyon, three studios including Criteria in Miami, the horn solo decided on a Saturday morning, and Robbie McIntosh's death weeks before the record reached No. 1. New chapter XI. Rust-and-tape palette. New instrument: the band roster, click to open.

  6. VI

    Kappa

    20 September 2026

    The site's own instrument turned on itself: chapter XII on the letter K, which classical Latin mostly abandoned and modern Italian keeps only as a foreign import, in a name that needs it twice, set in a typeface named for a man who worked his whole life in that same K-less language. New chapter, and a new instrument: a live specimen of the site's own variable K, its weight and optical size adjustable in the browser. Paper-and-cinnabar palette, a thick-and-hairline rule under the nav standing for Bodoni's own stroke contrast.

  7. VII

    Registration

    27 September 2026

    A line from chapter X followed up: Rafe Lavine's 2004 claim that a 1968 album by the British trio The Peddlers had “a cult following here in SA.” New chapter XIII traces the band from a Joe Meek session in 1963 through the Pickwick Club, Three in a Cell, and Roy Phillips's move to New Zealand in 1981, and finds a real, documented following there, a No. 1 single and a twelve-week tour, but nothing at all for South Africa beyond Lavine's own word. Petrol-teal and brass palette, a drawbar comb under the nav. New instrument: a working Hammond-style drawbar registration, set it and hold a button to hear it.

  8. VIII

    Heel

    4 October 2026

    The open question on the first name: where Kobus comes from and how far it travels. New chapter XIV follows the chain from the Hebrew Yaʿaqov, whose Biblical gloss is a heel, through Greek and Latin to Dutch Jacobus and the short form Kobus, lists seven other bearers that could be sourced, and separates the given name from the antelope genus. Research was limited to search extracts; the page lists what could not be found, among it the first written Kobus and any sourced figure for the name's frequency. Delft-blue palette, a tile border under the nav. New instrument: a slider that wears the name down along the Dutch road or the Romance road.

  9. IX

    Render

    5 October 2026 · a special edition

    Commissioned by the site’s owner, and the one edition that is not research. A phrase with no known origin, Pig Man Fat or PMF, was searched for in the archive and on the web and not found, so Volume IV invents a legend for it, labelled as invented on every page. Chapter XV tells it: a thin pig, a wolf, a jackal, the last pat of fat in a drought, and a trap that borrows its turn from Br’er Rabbit’s briar patch and its cast from the Cape’s Jackal and Wolf tales. Chapter XVI is an instrument that retells it through twelve invented tellers while the photographed figure grows. Chapter XVII is the real part: the traditions the tale borrows from, the fat face, rendering, and a list of what was not found. Lard-cream palette, a drip border under the nav, headings in Abril Fatface.

Open questions

Threads a future edition could pull. Each one is real, and each one is unanswered on these pages today.

  • What was Rafe Lavine's afternoon programme on SAfm, and in which years did it run?
  • Was the mystery-song feature really called Beat the Beat, and did it have rules, prizes or a regular slot?
  • What date was the broadcast? Ed, Trevor Worley, Martin Lake, Charles Mitchell and Ben were the other callers that day.
  • The Average White Band recorded Pick Up the Pieces at Criteria in Miami with Arif Mardin. What happened in that room, and who played the sax solo? Answered in Edition V, chapter XI: the album was cut across three studios, Clover in Hollywood, Criteria in Miami and Atlantic in New York, and the solo is Molly Duncan's, on tenor, chosen over Roger Ball's alto the morning after the rest of the track was recorded.
  • Kobus Wiese, Kobus Moolman, and the other Kobuses: how far does the name travel, and where did it start? Investigated in Edition VIII, chapter XIV: the chain from Yaʿaqov to Kobus is documented, seven further bearers are listed, but the first written Kobus and the earliest bearer at the Cape could not be found.
  • The Ó Cinnéide of Munster: how did a nephew of Brian Boru's clan become a surname carried to the Cape?
  • Bodoni Moda is the face of these pages. What did Giambattista Bodoni's K actually look like, and how close is this one? Investigated in Edition VI, chapter XII: no source describes a K cut by Bodoni himself, or confirms one appeared in his plain Italian roman specimens at all. What is confirmed instead is that standard Italian has no native K to begin with, so he may never have needed one; every digital revival, this site's own included, drew one anyway, for a market Bodoni never had to serve. What historical material Bodoni Moda's designer, Owen Earl, actually drew from, and how they drew the letters Bodoni's Italian founts never needed, remains open.
  • What did SAfm sound like in the 1990s, before the 2004 cull, and who else was on it?
  • The Peddlers' Three in a Cell got Lavine into jazz. Who were The Peddlers, and why did they have a cult following in South Africa? Investigated in Edition VII, chapter XIII: the trio and the album are real and well documented, but no chart entry, pressing, tour date or interview mention ties them to South Africa anywhere searched. Their one documented following abroad was in New Zealand.
  • Radio Today, 1485 AM, and The RockFest: what was community rock radio in Johannesburg in 2006?
  • Robbie McIntosh died in Los Angeles in September 1974. Sources split on the 22nd or the 23rd, and several add that Cher was at the same party. Which date is right, and does the Cher detail hold up against an earlier, better source?
  • Average White Band's own name is explained two ways in circulation, one about a jam session, one about the tropics. Which, if either, is the real story?
  • What became of Roger Ball, the band's saxophonist and the writer of the Pick Up the Pieces riff, after he left the band in 1997?
  • What does the title of the Peddlers' album Three in a Cell actually refer to: the trio, or the sleeve's cell-like cover? No source says.
  • Is Tab Martin, the Peddlers' bassist, still alive? He is last documented as having moved to Portugal.
  • Is Trevor Morais, the Peddlers' first drummer, still alive and working? His Wikipedia entry implies it but nothing dated confirms it.
  • When did the Peddlers actually disband? 1976 is reported only once, uncorroborated.
  • What is the figure in Volume IV’s plate made of, who made it, and where and when was the photograph taken? Not supplied, and not visible in the image.
  • Is Pig Man Fat, or PMF, a saying anywhere outside the family that uses it? Nothing was found in the archive or in four web searches, which does not prove there is nothing.
  • When is Kobus first written in a Dutch record, and who is the earliest bearer of Jacobus or Kobus at the Cape?
  • Is Kobus a short form or a diminutive of Jacobus, and does Koos come from Jacobus directly or by way of Kobus?
  • How common is Kobus today in South Africa, Namibia and the Netherlands? The Meertens Voornamenbank and Statistics South Africa could not be reached.
  • What is the Afrikaans pronunciation of Kobus, from a dictionary? Chapter II's transcription is unsourced; the only sourced IPA found is Dutch.
  • I give you my pink car? Added by the owner, verbatim; no context supplied.
  • light the Lampoon’s. Added by the owner, verbatim; no context supplied.
